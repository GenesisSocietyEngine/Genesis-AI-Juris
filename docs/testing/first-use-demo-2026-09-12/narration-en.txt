A useful decision tool should help you reach a clear next step, and explain why it makes sense. GENESIS: JURIS brings the case, the decision map and the analytical report into one workspace. In this walkthrough, we will start with a ready-made case, look at two ways to bring in your own material, make a visible edit and prepare a report. Our example is Project Canopy, a fictional managed-site expansion. Every organisation, person, document and figure in it is synthetic. Watch the first two minutes for the entry route, then stay for the complete authoring workflow. The interface opens in user view, with technical details available when needed.

For your own saved work, begin with the account screen. Sign in yourself using the available method, and complete the profile confirmation if this is your first visit. The recording pauses while personal information is entered; credentials never appear in the demo. Continue into the workspace and choose a case from the library. The title and short description help you decide where to begin. Open a scenario, read the immediate situation and inspect the available choices. You can start learning from a prepared case before authoring one of your own. For this demonstration, Canopy is opened from its prepared Base package. It asks a concrete question: should a team proceed with a managed-site expansion, authorize limited transition work, defer or decline? Keep that question in view as we move into Studio.

There are three practical starting points: a prepared scenario, a structured case file or a written brief. In Studio, use the import action for a supported JSON case, or for a Markdown or text prompt. A structured JSON case is checked before it replaces the current draft. A written brief enters the description stage, where you can read it before moving on. These routes serve different needs. Use a canonical case when you want to preserve a defined structure. Use a prompt when you are still framing the situation. For Canopy, keep the Base package and its source versions together. A failed import should leave your existing work in place. The aim is to move quickly from your material to a case you can inspect. Choose the starting point that fits the work already in front of you.

For the prompt route, write the decision, the people involved, the known facts and the questions still open. Specific inputs make the proposed structure easier to review. Where AI is available for your account, ask it to understand and structure the brief. Read the proposed interpretation before applying it. Look for facts that were changed, assumptions that need a source, and missing alternatives. The author remains responsible for what enters the case. An AI proposal is a starting point for review. The interface lets you examine changes before applying them as an authoring operation. If the service is unavailable, use the clearly labelled local option or continue editing manually. For this recorded example, the exact prepared Canopy package keeps the case consistent throughout. Any separate AI-generated result should be identified as a new proposal.

Now the case becomes a map you can inspect. The prepared Canopy Base contains fourteen nodes. Read the route from the initial situation through its evidence and decision checks to an outcome. Select a node to see its title and content. The purpose of the map is to expose the logic: what is known, what changes the decision and where a route ends. For a simple editing demonstration, select a connection and press Delete. The link disappears visibly. Undo brings it back, so you can experiment without losing the intended route. Keep the labels readable and make one change at a time. The user view keeps the main actions close to the work. Developer view remains available for detailed settings and diagnostics, but is not required to understand the case.

A decision also uses time and resources. Select the relevant node and inspect its budget cost and duration. Give those values an explicit meaning in the case, so another reader can follow the assumptions. Canopy provides a simple economic illustration: setup costs of two hundred and forty thousand credits and annual contribution of one hundred and twenty thousand imply a simple payback of two years. These are fictional declared inputs, not operating results. The arithmetic is easy to inspect, and its limitations remain visible. A promising payback does not close an evidence gap or authorize production. In a tax-structure case, review the dedicated economic inputs and the calculated results in their own context. Keep profitability, cash flow and payback definitions clear, and retain the required substantive review before any external use.

Before testing, use the checks to find missing pieces and return to the relevant point on the map. A complete route needs a clear beginning and an outcome. Then test the case and follow the decisions as a reader would. Canopy makes the evidence distinction concrete. Its Base demand document describes six hundred indicated packs per week, but only three hundred are signed commitments. Evidenced capacity is four hundred and eighty. Those figures cannot be treated as interchangeable. The Base branch therefore concerns conditional transition work, with unresolved evidence and leadership requirements; it does not authorize production. A useful demonstration shows why a branch was taken and what still has to change. Keep the underlying source version attached to the claim. This gives a reviewer something specific to challenge, correct or confirm, instead of a conclusion detached from its inputs.

When the draft is ready for discussion, choose Generate report. The report dialog puts Preview PDF and Download PDF at the top, so you can see the result before exploring advanced settings. Start with a preliminary report for internal review. The document brings together the executive summary, decision map, checks and the selected registers and economic assumptions. Review the page layout, names, numbers and full text alternative to the graph. Open the settings when you need to adjust the audience, classification or included material. The original AI prompt is not included in the report. A downloaded preliminary PDF is a working deliverable; it does not establish independent approval. Governed outputs and the reviewer's decision belong to the separate case-report workflow, with their own conditions. For a first session, the useful result is concrete: a case you can inspect and a clearly labelled analytical draft that another person can read and question.

The first-use journey should leave you with three things: a case you understand, a visible decision structure and a report you can share for the right kind of review. A ready-made scenario is the quickest way to explore. A canonical file carries an existing structure into Studio. A prompt helps you begin when the material is still unstructured. From there, review the assumptions, edit the map and create the analytical draft. For a team evaluating the product, choose one recurring decision and try this workflow with synthetic or sanitized material. Measure the time to first useful action, the questions raised and the effort needed to prepare the report. The next step is simple: open a case and make one decision that you can explain.
