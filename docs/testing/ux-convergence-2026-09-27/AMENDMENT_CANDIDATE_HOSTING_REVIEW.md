## Authentication limits that determine the candidate audience

Sites dispatch owns `/signin-with-chatgpt`, `/signout-with-chatgpt` and `/callback`. `getChatGPTUser` consumes trusted Sites identity headers **before** considering the ordinary local session cookie. Password enrollment requires a trusted ChatGPT identity; local sessions use the existing Secure/HttpOnly `__Host` cookie and strict same-origin mutations.

Therefore a sole-viewer owner-private Site can establish owner-side hosted behavior, but it cannot be assumed to expose a guest flow or to exercise other actors simply by entering their local passwords while the owner's trusted provider identity remains active. That would be a different identity context, not valid role acceptance. The private gateway's unauthenticated behavior must be observed and labelled separately from the current public product's anonymous entry.

For multi-actor acceptance, actual approved QA provider identities must be permitted at the Site gateway and then obtain the application's intended synthetic organization/case roles through ordinary authorized paths. Current production group ownership versus shared-personal membership is not that setup. A custom shared review audience may support signed-in role checks; a guest/public-origin check needs a deliberately authorized audience that actually exposes it. Do not silently widen an audience, forge trusted headers, reuse production session material, or use the available SIWC bypass-token capability as authentication proof.
